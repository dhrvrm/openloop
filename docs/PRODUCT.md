# ADHD-Centered Product Definition

## Definition

OpenLoop ADHD is a personal external executive-function layer. It reduces the
cognitive work required to hold intent, choose a next action, begin, survive an
interruption, and recover relevant memory.

It is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat ADHD. It is designed
around recurring ADHD-related functional problems without making health claims.

## The user

The product is initially for its developer and people with similar patterns:

- working memory is unreliable under stress or interruption;
- thoughts arrive faster than they can be organized;
- large or ambiguous work creates initiation resistance;
- reminders become noise when they lack context;
- time estimates and future obligations do not remain salient;
- anxiety increases avoidance and avoidance increases anxiety;
- restarting after an interruption costs more energy than the work itself.

There is no buyer persona, organization administrator, or team workflow in the
current product.

## Product promise

> OpenLoop keeps hold of your intention while your attention moves elsewhere,
> then gives it back in a form you can actually resume.

## Product pillars

### Frictionless externalization

One global shortcut opens an empty capture surface. The user can type or,
eventually, speak without selecting a project, due date, tag, or task type.
Capture closes instantly and never blocks on AI processing.

### Action clarification

The application distinguishes among:

- something to do;
- something to remember;
- something to consider later;
- an emotional or mental unload that requires no action;
- an unclear item that should remain unforced.

For actionable items it proposes one small physical next action. It never turns
every sentence into a task.

### Task activation

Starting work presents only:

- the current intention;
- the smallest next action;
- the information required to begin;
- a visible but calm elapsed-time cue;
- pause, interrupt, and finish controls.

It does not show an entire backlog while the person is trying to start.

### Interruption recovery

Before switching away, OpenLoop records a restart packet:

- what the person was trying to achieve;
- what they just completed;
- the exact next action;
- open files, links, or notes when locally available;
- the uncertainty or blocker;
- the time the state was captured.

Returning to work opens this packet rather than asking the user to reconstruct
their mental state.

### Prospective memory

Items resurface by usable context—not just date and clock time. Context can
include application, document, person, location category, current focus, or
elapsed time. All sensing remains local and configurable.

### Evidence-backed recall

The user can ask what they intended, decided, promised, or previously learned.
Answers display their source and confidence. The system prefers “I do not have
enough evidence” to a plausible invention.

### Anxiety-safe interaction

The product has no shame language, red overdue counts, streaks, productivity
scores, or accumulating notification debt. Missing something changes the plan;
it does not punish the person.

## Core screens

Only five surfaces are permitted initially:

1. **Quick Capture** — one field, optional voice button, no organization step.
2. **Now** — one intention, next action, context, and recovery controls.
3. **Return** — interruption snapshots that can be resumed or dismissed.
4. **Later** — a quiet holding area surfaced selectively, not an inbox counter.
5. **Recall** — local search and evidence-backed questions.

The menu bar always exposes Capture, Now, Pause, and Private Mode.

## Success measurements

The first measurements are personal and behavioral:

- thoughts captured before they disappear;
- captured thoughts later recovered;
- actions begun within two minutes of selecting them;
- interrupted sessions successfully resumed;
- reminders dismissed because they were irrelevant;
- generated next actions corrected by the user;
- days the application remains enabled without becoming irritating.

Transcription hours, token counts, task volume, and time spent in the application
are not success metrics.

## Explicit exclusions

- accounts and authentication;
- organization or collaboration features;
- cloud storage or synchronization;
- website or hosted backend;
- subscriptions, billing, or licensing;
- employee observation;
- medical diagnosis or therapeutic claims;
- general project management;
- mandatory daily planning rituals;
- a chatbot as the primary interface;
- voice transcription as the product identity.
